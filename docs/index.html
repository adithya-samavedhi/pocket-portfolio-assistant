Research assistant

Ask about a company. Get an answer that cites its sources.

It reads SEC filings and live market data, decides which of those a question actually needs, and reconciles them into one answer — with a link to every figure it used.

What happens when you ask

Four steps, about five seconds. The order matters: nothing is retrieved until the question has been understood, and nothing is answered until the evidence is in.

1

Your question is read, not matched

It works out which sources could actually answer you, and rewrites your question for each one. Filings can't tell you whether a stock is expensive; they can tell you what revenue and margins did.

"Is NVDA expensive?" → filings are asked about growth and margins, market data about the multiple

2

The right specialists work — at the same time

One, both, or neither. They run in parallel, so consulting two costs barely more time than consulting one.

Filings

Searches the 10-K and 10-Q text for passages that bear on the question.

Market

Pulls the quote, the multiples, and the trend measures for the ticker.

3

Each returns a summary, never a document dump

A specialist may read fifty pages; it hands back a short finding plus the sources behind it. That keeps the final step reading evidence rather than drowning in it.

4

The findings are reconciled, not stapled together

Where the filings and the market point different ways, the answer says so explicitly instead of presenting a contradiction as agreement. Every figure keeps the citation it came from.

The two specialists

They are deliberately separate, because they are good at different questions and wrong in different ways.

Filings

What the company reported

  • Revenue, segments, margins
  • Named risk factors
  • Management's own commentary
  • How any of it changed across quarters

SEC 10-K and 10-Q · quarters labelled the company's way (NVDA's July quarter is Q2 FY2026, not Q3 2025)

Market

What the stock has done

  • Price, market cap, P/E and P/S
  • Momentum from one month to one year
  • Position in the 52-week range
  • Drawdown and volatility

Every number computed from the full price series before it reaches the model — the model interprets, it never does the arithmetic

A real answer

A question that needs both sources, and what comes back.

Is NVDA expensive right now?

consulted filings market

Market data shows NVDA trading at $223.96, near its 52-week high of $235.47[1][3]. Multiples are elevated — a price-to-sales ratio of 21.4 and a trailing P/E of 33.58[2] — though the forward P/E compresses to 17.37[2], a tension between today's valuation and expected earnings growth. The filings show growth that supports those multiples: revenue and net income each rose 65%, to $215.9bn and $120.1bn, at a 71.1% gross margin[4]. No historical P/E series was available, so this is not a comparison against NVDA's own valuation history[1].

What it will refuse to do

The useful part of a research tool is knowing where it stops.

  • It won't answer about a company it doesn't hold. Ask about Tesla or Meta and it declines rather than reaching for the nearest company it does have. "No specialist covers this question. I can answer for AAPL, AMZN, GOOGL, MSFT and NVDA."
  • It won't advise or predict. "Should I buy?" and "will it go up next week?" have no source behind them, so they get declined instead of dressed up.
  • It says when the evidence isn't there. If the passages it found don't answer the question, it reports that — with no citations and low confidence — rather than filling the gap.
  • It won't invent a comparison it can't make. Historical valuation multiples aren't in its sources, so it names that limitation instead of implying a trend from today's number.

Using it

A browser window, or the command line if you prefer.

In the browser

# start it, then open http://127.0.0.1:8000
./venv/bin/python src/web.py

You'll see which specialists were chosen and why, watch each one report as it finishes, then get the reconciled answer with its sources.

From the terminal

./venv/bin/python src/orchestrator.py "Is NVDA expensive right now?"

# add -v to watch the routing and timings
./venv/bin/python src/orchestrator.py "How has Microsoft's margin changed?" -v
